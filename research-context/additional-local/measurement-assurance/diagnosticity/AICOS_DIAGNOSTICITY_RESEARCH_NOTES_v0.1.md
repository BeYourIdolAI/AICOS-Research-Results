## Why the gates exist

D1 SAME TARGET:
Motivated by AT1 arm mismatch. It prevents conservative and stronger outputs
from being compared as if they were the same claim.

D2 SINGLE AXIS:
Motivated by AT1 varying information state and attempted attribution together.

D3 EXPLICIT DEPENDENCY:
Motivated by AT1 §15.1 rad 522 antecedent failure and AT4 dependency
problems. It prevents unstated Q→P or prerequisite relations from carrying a
result.

D4 CONTROL VIABILITY:
Motivated by AT4 control failure and bridge issue. A blocked control cannot
support attack discrimination.

D5 ATTACK-SENSITIVE BLOCKER:
Motivated by orthogonal blocker patterns. A blocker must differ between arms
to evidence the attack axis.

D6 NON-TAUTOLOGICAL DISCRIMINATION:
Motivated by AT3 §12 rad 457 fixture construction. Varying the exact predicate
named by the rule guarantees discrimination by construction.

D7 NO CONCLUSION STIPULATION:
Motivated by AT3 treatment of fixture facts and non-novelty. A fixture cannot
stipulate the discovery result.

D8 TYPE PRESERVATION:
Motivated by repeated risks of treating claims, evidence, assessments and
metadata as interchangeable.

D9 SEMANTIC COMPLETENESS:
Motivated by the AT4 §15.1 × §16.1 semantic fork. An unresolved relation may
make the result AMBIGUOUS or INVALID TRACE.

D10 OUTCOME-LAYER SEPARATION:
Motivated by repeated conflation of normative conformance, detectability and
runtime prevention.

D11 FALSIFIABILITY:
Motivated by AT3 discrimination guaranteed by construction. A fixture cannot
serve as falsification if every consistent world produces the same result.
